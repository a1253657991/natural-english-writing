# Genre profiles

Use these in Phase 3 only when the user has no sample. The venue's own guidelines, rubric or template override them. Each profile gives what the reader expects, the register settings, and the typical drift of generated text in that genre.

## Research article (empirical: economics, social science, applied linguistics)

- **Reader:** specialists judging the contribution, the method and the evidence.
- **Moves:** Introduction: establish the territory, show the gap (citing who left it), state how this paper fills it (Swales's CARS model). Methods: enough to replicate. Results: findings with numbers. Discussion: interpretation, comparison with earlier work, limitations, implications.
- **Register:** "we", or "I" for single authors where the field accepts it (check the venue). Passive common in methods. Past tense for your own procedures and results; present tense for established knowledge and for what the paper does. Hedge interpretations ("suggests", "is consistent with"), not reported numbers. Choose reporting verbs for stance ("argue", "show", "claim", "find").
- **Generated drift:** inflated contribution ("novel", "comprehensive"), gap statements with no citation, a discussion that repeats the results, symmetric lists of implications, a weak authorial position.

## Abstract

- One paragraph of 150–300 words unless the venue uses structured abstracts. At most one sentence of background, then aim, data and method, main result with numbers, implication. Usually no citations.
- **Generated drift:** several sentences of background, results without numbers, a closing sentence about "valuable insights".

## Coursework essay or assignment (argumentative or analytical)

- **Reader:** a marker applying a rubric, usually an academic in the field.
- **Moves:** thesis early; each paragraph advances one argument with evidence and analysis; counter-argument where the rubric rewards it; a conclusion that answers the question.
- **Register:** formal. First person depends on the discipline and task (reflective tasks usually allow "I"). Citation style per the unit guide. Australian or British spelling at Australian universities unless told otherwise.
- **Generated drift:** an introduction that restates the question, "This essay will explore…", a balanced survey with no position, paragraphs ending in summary sentences, triads.

## Reflective writing

- "I"; past tense for events, present tense for current understanding; specific incidents; experience linked to course concepts; honesty about what is still unclear.
- **Generated drift:** generic lessons ("I learned the importance of teamwork") with no concrete incident.

## Personal statement, statement of purpose, scholarship essay

- **Reader:** a committee reading many applications, looking for fit, specific motivation and evidence of ability.
- **Register:** "I"; concrete experiences with outcomes; plain verbs; modest, specific claims; links to the programme's actual features (from the user, verified).
- **Generated drift:** "passion", "journey", "ever since I was young", lists of adjectives about oneself, inflated significance, generic praise of the institution.

## Cover letter or job application answer

- **Reader:** a recruiter or hiring manager, often skimming, matching against the role's requirements.
- **Register:** "I"; short paragraphs; evidence with numbers (situation, task, action, result for competency questions); the role's own terms, used honestly.
- **Generated drift:** "I am excited to apply", "proven track record", "leverage my skills", three-adjective self-descriptions, a closing paragraph that restates enthusiasm.

## Business report, consulting memo, investment note

- **Reader:** a decision-maker with little time.
- **Moves:** answer first (the recommendation or finding), then supporting points in order of importance, then detail. Headings that state findings ("Margins fell because input costs rose") rather than topics.
- **Register:** "we" or the firm's name; active voice; numbers with units, dates and sources. Bullets are fine for parallel items with real content; tables for comparisons.
- **Generated drift:** scene-setting openings, topic-only headings, bold-label bullets with little content, "key takeaways" that repeat the body, vague risk lists.

## Policy brief

- Problem, evidence, options with their costs, recommendation. Plain words for non-specialist decision-makers; terms defined; sources cited.
- **Generated drift:** "a multifaceted challenge", unnamed stakeholders, recommendations without an owner or a cost.

## Professional email

- Purpose in the first line; request and deadline explicit; one topic per email; a conventional greeting and sign-off.
- **Generated drift:** "I hope this email finds you well" followed by context the reader already has; a closing offer of further help.
